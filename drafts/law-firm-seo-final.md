## BODY CONTENT (H2 sections)

### What law firm SEO actually costs

The national average retainer is about $7,500 a month (Lexicon Legal Content, 2026). That hides a wide spread.

Solo and small firms typically pay $2,500 to $5,000. At that level you get roughly eight pages of content a month plus basic technical maintenance. Most other firms land between $3,000 and $15,000. Tier-one metropolitan personal injury markets run $15,000 to $30,000 and up.

Below about $1,500 a month, be careful. Agencies quoting far under these ranges are either leaving out necessary work or using tactics that carry penalty risk.

And any agency that quotes a monthly figure without auditing your site first is guessing.

### The question most firms forget to ask

Who owns your website, your domain and your content if you cancel?

Get it in writing, not in the sales call. Published estimates put the cost of rebuilding after leaving Scorpion — one of the largest legal marketing agencies — at $10,000 to $50,000 (Intercore Technologies, 2026). Firms find this out at exactly the wrong moment: after they've decided to leave.

Ask before you sign. A good agency won't mind the question.

### Check who actually owns the agency

Agencies get acquired, and the reputation you're buying may belong to people who left.

Nifty Marketing is the clearest example. Almost every "best legal SEO" list describes it through founder Mike Ramsey's work in local search. Ramsey sold the agency to Rockbridge Growth Equity in 2020 and is listed as former president on his own LinkedIn.

That's not a reason to avoid them. It is a reason to ask who's running things now, and who'll be running your account.

### Why legal SEO isn't normal SEO

Three things make it different.

**State bar advertising rules.** Every state limits what you can claim. An agency that doesn't know those rules can write content that ranks and still gets you in trouble.

**YMYL standards.** Google holds "your money or your life" content — legal, medical, financial — to higher quality standards. Thin content that works elsewhere doesn't work here.

**The map pack decides local cases.** For most practice areas, the local three-pack matters more than the organic results below it. If your Google Business Profile is weak, your blog won't save you.

### Judge agencies on cases, not traffic

LawRank makes a point worth stealing whoever you hire: don't buy traffic, buy signed cases.

Ignore agencies showing off page-one rankings and traffic charts. Ask for call volume and signed cases. If they can't show those, they're selling vanity metrics.

### Ask about AI search, and listen carefully

About 48% of tracked Google queries now trigger AI Overviews, up from 31% a year earlier (BrightEdge, February 2026). ChatGPT referral traffic grew 1,079% year over year in one analysis of 94 brands (Visibility Labs, 2025).

So ask every agency: when someone asks ChatGPT for my practice area in my city, does my firm come up, and what specifically will you do about it?

The answer tells you whether they're working in 2026 or still selling 2019.

### Why most lists of legal SEO agencies are unreliable

Nearly every "best law firm SEO agency" article is published by a law firm SEO agency.

Rankings.io publishes one. Consultwebs publishes one. LawRank publishes one. First Page Sage publishes one. NEWMEDIA publishes one. Grow Law publishes one. Most put themselves at or near the top.

Some disclose it, to their credit — Juris Digital's founder says openly that his agency appears on his own list.

We don't sell SEO to law firms. That's the only reason this list can be ordered by fit rather than by who owns the domain.

## FAQ

**How much should a law firm spend on SEO?**
Between $2,500 and $5,000 a month for solo and small firms, and $3,000 to $15,000 for most others. Competitive personal injury markets run $15,000 to $30,000 or more. The national average is around $7,500.

**How long before SEO produces cases?**
Most agencies ask for a 6 to 12 month commitment, and that's honest rather than a sales tactic. Technical fixes can move things in weeks. Signed cases take longer.

**Is a legal specialist better than a generalist?**
For law firms, usually yes. An agency splitting attention between law firms, dentists and HVAC companies hasn't built the same muscle. State bar rules and YMYL standards are specialist knowledge.

**What's the biggest red flag?**
Guaranteed rankings. Nobody controls Google's results. An agency promising specific positions is either misinforming you or planning something risky.

**Why are there only six agencies on this list?**
Because these are the ones whose details we could verify. We confirmed each agency's ownership, founders and location from primary sources, and left out several well-known names whose claims we couldn't check. We'll add more as we verify them.

**What is GEO, and do I need it?**
Generative engine optimisation means getting cited in AI answers from ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI Overviews. It isn't really a separate budget line — it's a different way of directing what you already spend. Ask how an agency approaches it.
