## BODY CONTENT (rich text — H2/H3)

### Why most "best SaaS SEO agency" lists can't be trusted

Search "best SaaS SEO agencies" and nearly every result is published by an SEO agency. Onely's guide ranks Onely first — to their credit, they disclose it openly. Powered by Search, First Page Sage, Embarque and DerivateX all appear in lists they publish themselves. TripleDart's ranking lives on TripleDart's website.

This isn't necessarily dishonest, and some of those lists are genuinely useful. But it does mean the "consensus" you're reading is largely agencies citing each other, and the top slot is frequently occupied by whoever owns the domain.

We don't sell SEO services. This list is built on which agencies are named repeatedly by sources that have no stake in the answer, then segmented by what each one is actually best at.

### What SaaS SEO actually costs in 2026

Most lists omit pricing entirely, which makes them close to useless for shortlisting. Here are the real bands.

**$3,000–$8,000/month** buys foundational work: keyword strategy, roughly four to ten content pieces a month, basic technical SEO and light link building. This is the right band for pre-seed through Series A, and the right expectation is validating whether organic is a viable channel — not scaling it.

**$8,000–$15,000/month** is where most growth-stage SaaS lands. At this level you get full content strategy and production of roughly fifteen to twenty-five pieces monthly, dedicated link building or digital PR, conversion optimisation, and increasingly generative engine optimisation for AI search.

**$15,000+/month** covers enterprise programmes with full-funnel demand generation, and can run past $30,000 for large-scale work.

Below $3,000/month you are buying one channel, not a programme. Broader market data puts the average agency retainer at around $3,200/month across all industries, but SaaS sits above that because the work requires category positioning, comparison pages, use-case content and close coordination with product marketing.

### How to choose by stage

**Pre-seed to Series A.** Your question isn't "which agency is best" — it's "is organic search viable for us at all." Pick a lean specialist, cap the budget, and set a six-month checkpoint with a specific success metric. Embarque and SimpleTiger's entry tier are built for this.

**Series B to C.** Now you're building an engine. This is where content quality compounds and where the premium agencies earn their fee. Omniscient Digital if quality is the priority, Grow and Convert if conversion is the gap, Skale if signup volume is the metric.

**Enterprise.** SEO stops being a channel and becomes one input into demand generation across a long sales cycle and a buying committee. Powered by Search and Directive operate at this level.

### The questions that actually separate agencies

Ask for SaaS case studies **at your ARR stage** — a great result for a $50M company tells you nothing about your seed-stage site. Ask for pipeline metrics rather than traffic: demos, signups, qualified leads. Ask them to walk you through a currently-ranking article for a past client and explain how it converts. Ask explicitly how they approach AI search visibility, since buyers increasingly start in ChatGPT and Perplexity rather than Google. And insist on meeting the strategist who will actually own your account, not just the one who shows up to the pitch.

One warning sign worth taking seriously: an agency that treats your SaaS product like a local business or an ecommerce store will target keywords with no commercial intent, and you'll get traffic that never converts.

### How long this takes

The first three months are foundational — audits, fixes, strategy. Early ranking movement typically shows around months four to six. Measurable traffic and lead increases usually land between months six and twelve, with full return often taking twelve to eighteen months. Any agency promising first-position rankings in thirty days is telling you something useful about themselves.

## FAQ

**How much should a SaaS company spend on SEO?**
Between $3,000 and $8,000 a month at seed to Series A, $8,000 to $15,000 at growth stage, and $15,000 or more for enterprise programmes. Below $3,000 a month you're funding a single channel rather than a complete programme, which is usually a false economy.

**Is a SaaS-specialist agency actually better than a generalist?**
For most SaaS companies, yes — mainly because generalists tend to target high-volume keywords with no commercial intent, and because SaaS sites have specific technical problems (JavaScript rendering, indexation gaps on product pages, login walls hiding pricing pages, cannibalisation across blog and docs) that generalists frequently miss.

**How long before SEO produces pipeline?**
Expect six to twelve months for measurable traffic and lead increases, and twelve to eighteen for full return. Technical fixes can show movement in weeks; revenue impact does not.

**What is GEO, and does it matter yet?**
Generative engine optimisation is optimising to appear in AI-generated answers from ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google's AI Overviews. It matters now and is becoming a standard part of SaaS SEO retainers — if an agency has no answer on it, that tells you something about how current their thinking is.

**Should I hire an agency or build in-house?**
For most Series A and Series B companies, an agency delivers better output per dollar in the first two years. Once SEO becomes a core growth channel, a hybrid model — agency plus an in-house lead — tends to make more sense than either alone.

**Can I trust agency case studies?**
Treat them as marketing until verified. Ask for the client's name, the timeframe, the starting baseline, and a live URL you can check yourself. Percentage growth figures without a baseline are close to meaningless — 2,500% growth from a hundred visitors is a hundred visitors.
